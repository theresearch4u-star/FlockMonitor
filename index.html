Species under pressure

Key birds — migration, population and 5 facts each

🐦
Greater Crested Tern
1,000+ dead · SA mass mortality · Highest impact
  • 1. Found along the full Australian coastline and offshore islands; nests in dense colonies.
  • 2. Breeding season typically October–December; nests packed so tightly birds can touch bills.
  • 3. Mostly resident/coastal — does not make long ocean migrations like petrels or skuas.
  • 4. IUCN Least Concern globally, but dense colonies make it highly vulnerable to rapid H5 spread.
  • 5. Has become the main local amplifier species in Australia’s current outbreak.
🐧
Little Penguin
Confirmed death · ~5,000 being vaccinated · Priority protection
  • 1. World’s smallest penguin — about 33–45 cm tall and ~1 kg.
  • 2. Phillip Island holds one of the largest colonies; population recovered strongly after fox control and habitat work.
  • 3. Returns to the same burrow most nights; forages 14–20 km daily in inshore waters.
  • 4. Can dive to ~30 m (max ~70 m) and stay underwater over a minute.
  • 5. First Australian H5 death on Phillip Island; King Island birds under test; major vaccine program underway.
🦅
Brown Skua
Early detections · WA & TAS · Entry pathway
  • 1. Breeds on sub-Antarctic and Antarctic islands; spends the non-breeding season at sea further north.
  • 2. Regular winter visitor to southern Australian waters; rarely comes ashore unless sick or exhausted.
  • 3. Long-distance migrant — individuals can travel thousands of kilometres from breeding grounds.
  • 4. One of the first H5-positive birds found in Australia (Western Australia, June 2026).
  • 5. Strongly linked to the sub-Antarctic introduction pathway into Australia.
🕊️
Southern Giant Petrel
Migratory vector · Early SA & WA cases
  • 1. One of the largest petrels; breeds on sub-Antarctic islands including Macquarie and Heard.
  • 2. World population roughly 60,000+ individuals; several thousand breeding pairs in Australian territory.
  • 3. Wide-ranging Southern Ocean migrant; visits Australian southern waters outside the breeding season.
  • 4. Rarely comes ashore on the mainland unless in poor condition — early H5 cases were birds found sick on beaches.
  • 5. Among the first confirmed H5 detections in Australia and a key suspected vector from the south.

Other detections include silver gulls and additional seabirds. Data reflects public reports as of late August 2026.

How it spreads

Four pathways driving the outbreak

1. Migratory birds

Skuas, petrels and terns carry the virus across oceans. Early Australian cases were linked to the sub-Antarctic pathway.

2. Dense colonies

Close nesting and shared water mean one infected bird can expose many others quickly — especially in tern and penguin colonies.

3. Environment

Virus in faeces, water and shoreline can remain infectious for days. Cool, wet coastal conditions favour survival.

4. Mass mortality

When the virus hits a naïve colony, deaths can rise rapidly. The 1,000+ crested terns in SA are a clear example.

Human risk remains low. Transmission to people requires close contact with infected birds or their environments.

How • Why • When

How it spreads

Migratory seabirds carry the virus. Dense colonies and scavenging amplify transmission on coasts and islands.

Why now

H5N1 reached the Australian mainland in June 2026. Southern seabird-rich coastlines provided ideal conditions for establishment.

When the peaks hit

Mainland June → Tasmania mid-August → mass mortality events now appearing. Further reports expected in coming weeks.

Impact Layers

Beyond case counts — what H5 is actually affecting.

Wildlife

High mortality risk for colonial seabirds and little penguins. Government assessment: nearly 1 in 5 native birds & mammals rated high to extremely high risk. Mass die-offs now occurring.

Tourism & Economy

Little penguin colonies are major visitor attractions. Colony losses or temporary closures threaten local jobs and the nature-based tourism brand.

Ecosystems

Seabirds and penguins play key roles in nutrient cycling. Large-scale losses on remote breeding islands can cascade through coastal food webs.

Agriculture

Zero detections in poultry or commercial production systems so far. Strict biosecurity is holding the line — the buffer remains thin and critical.

Human Health

Risk to the general public remains low. Avoid contact with sick or dead wild birds. Keep pets away from carcasses. Report via the national hotline.

Communities

Distress is real where beloved species are affected. Clear, timely information reduces anxiety and improves official reporting quality.
