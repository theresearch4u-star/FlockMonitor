How • Why • When

How it spreads

Migratory seabirds carry the virus. Dense colonies and scavenging amplify transmission on coasts and islands.

Why now

H5N1 reached the Australian mainland in June 2026. Southern seabird-rich coastlines provided ideal conditions for establishment.

When the peaks hit

Mainland June → Tasmania mid-August → mass mortality events now appearing. Further reports expected in coming weeks.

Focus

28 little penguins and 3 crested terns found dead at Port of Grassy (19–20 Aug).

Samples collected for H5 testing. Carcasses being removed to limit further spread. Specialist teams surged to the area.

Tasmania has confirmed H5 cases in wild birds. Officials expect further reports along the north-west coast and King Island.

Impact Layers

Beyond case counts — what H5 is actually affecting.

Wildlife

High mortality risk for colonial seabirds and little penguins. Government assessment: nearly 1 in 5 native birds & mammals rated high to extremely high risk. Mass die-offs now occurring.

Tourism & Economy

Little penguin colonies are major visitor attractions. Colony losses or temporary closures threaten local jobs and the nature-based tourism brand.

Ecosystems

Seabirds and penguins play key roles in nutrient cycling. Large-scale losses on remote breeding islands can cascade through coastal food webs.

Agriculture

Zero detections in poultry or commercial production systems so far. Strict biosecurity is holding the line — the buffer remains thin and critical.

Human Health

Risk to the general public remains low. Avoid contact with sick or dead wild birds. Keep pets away from carcasses. Report via the national hotline.

Communities

Distress is real where beloved species are affected. Clear, timely information reduces anxiety and improves official reporting quality.
