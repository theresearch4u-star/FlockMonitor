Tasmania 6+ events

King Island: 28 little penguins + 3 crested terns found dead (Port of Grassy). Testing ongoing. Specialist teams deployed.

Also: WA 10 • NSW 9 • QLD 1

Impact Layers

Wildlife

High risk to colonial seabirds & little penguins. Mass die-offs now occurring. ~1 in 5 native species rated high–extremely high risk.

Tourism

Penguin colonies are major attractions. Colony losses or closures threaten local economies and visitor experiences.

Agriculture

Zero detections in poultry to date. Strict biosecurity and surveillance continuing. Buffer remains critical.

Human Health

Official risk to general public remains low. Do not handle sick or dead birds. Report via 1800 675 888.

How • Why • When

How: Migratory seabirds carry the virus. Dense colonies + scavenging amplify spread on coasts and islands.

Why now: H5N1 reached the Australian mainland in June 2026. Southern seabird-rich coasts provided ideal conditions.

When: First mainland case June 2026 → Tasmania mid-August → mass mortality events appearing. Further reports expected.

What you can do

  • • Do not touch sick or dead wild birds
  • • Report multiple sick/dead birds: 1800 675 888 (24 hr)
  • • Keep cats indoors and dogs on leads near affected areas
  • • Follow official state biosecurity advice
